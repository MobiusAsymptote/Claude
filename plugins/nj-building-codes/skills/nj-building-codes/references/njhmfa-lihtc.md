# NJHMFA Developer Requirements and LIHTC Reference

## New Jersey Housing and Mortgage Finance Agency (NJHMFA)

### Overview
NJHMFA is the primary state agency for affordable housing financing in New Jersey, administering federal and state funding programs for rental and for-sale affordable housing production.

## NJHMFA Financing Programs

### 1. Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC)

**Federal authority**: Internal Revenue Code § 42; administered by IRS
**State allocation**: NJHMFA allocates NJ's annual LIHTC cap per Qualified Allocation Plan (QAP)

#### Income and Rent Requirements
| Test | Income Limit | Minimum Unit % |
|---|---|---|
| 20/50 test | ≤50% AMI | 20% of all units |
| 40/60 test | ≤60% AMI | 40% of all units |
| Average income test | Blended (20%–80%) | 40% at various income levels |

- Rent maximum: 30% of qualifying income for unit's income tier
- Utilities included: If tenant pays utilities, deduct utility allowance from rent maximum

#### Compliance Period
| Period | Duration | Consequence of Non-Compliance |
|---|---|---|
| Federal compliance period | 15 years | Tax credit recapture by IRS |
| Extended use period | 15+ years (30 total typical) | NJHMFA agreement enforcement |
| NJHMFA QAP affordability | 30 years (minimum) | Loss of state allocation eligibility |

#### 2024 QAP Key Requirements
- Current QAP: https://www.nj.gov/dca/hmfa/developers/docs/lihtc/qap/tc_qap_2024.pdf
- Annual QAP cycle; new round announced each spring
- Geographic set-asides: ~40% credits for Transit-Oriented Use Magnets (TUMs); remainder statewide

#### QAP Scoring Priorities (2024)
- **ENERGY STAR certification** — threshold requirement (not scored; mandatory)
- **Green/sustainable design** — competitive points above ENERGY STAR
- **Accessibility beyond minimums** — extra points for exceeding FHA/Section 504
- **Proximity to transit, services, employment** — location scoring
- **Preservation vs. new construction** — preservation often scored higher in competitive rounds
- **Supportive housing services** — points for on-site service amenities
- **Local government support** — resolution of support adds points
- **Developer experience** — track record in affordable housing

#### Developer Eligibility
- Private developers, municipalities, housing authorities, non-profit organizations
- **Training requirement**: Certificate of completion of housing development training course required
- No prior HUD/NJHMFA defaults or debarments
- Financial capacity demonstrated (balance sheet, prior project performance)

### 4. HOME Investment Partnerships Program (Federal / HUD)

**Federal authority**: 42 U.S.C. § 12721; 24 CFR Part 92
**Administered in NJ by**: NJHMFA (for rental) and local Participating Jurisdictions (PJs)

#### Income Requirements
| Household Income | Required Unit % |
|---|---|
| ≤60% AMI | 90% of HOME-assisted families |
| ≤50% AMI | 20% of units in projects with 5+ assisted units |
| ≤80% AMI | Maximum income for any assisted household |

#### Affordability Period
| Activity | Minimum Affordability |
|---|---|
| New construction rental | **20 years** |
| Substantial rehabilitation rental | 15 years |
| Homebuyer assistance | 5–15 years (amount-based) |

#### Matching Requirement
- **$0.25 per $1.00 federal HOME dollar** from non-federal sources
- Match sources: donated land, labor, materials, bond proceeds, other non-federal funding

#### CHDO Set-Aside
- Minimum **15%** of HOME allocation for Community Housing Development Organizations
- CHDO must be approved by Participating Jurisdiction

#### HOME Accessibility Requirements
- Fair Housing Act compliance required
- Section 504 (5% + 2%) applies if project receives other federal assistance
- Common areas accessible per ADA standards
- HOME funds alone do not trigger Section 504 (24 CFR 92.252(d) note; confirm with HUD)

## ENERGY STAR Requirement

### Applicability
- **Mandatory threshold requirement** for all NJHMFA LIHTC applications
- Applies to new construction and substantial rehabilitation
- Must be obtained before Certificate of Occupancy (CO); cannot be retroactive

### ENERGY STAR New Homes (Multifamily)
- Version: ENERGY STAR Multifamily New Construction (ESMFNC) — current version 1.2
- Alternative: ENERGY STAR Certified Homes Version 3.2 (for 1–3 story buildings)
- Certifying party: RESNET-accredited HERS Rater or EPA-recognized Verification Organization

### Performance Requirements
- Minimum 15–30% more energy-efficient than code-compliant building
- Envelope, mechanical systems, and lighting specifications
- Pre-construction design review + mid-construction + final inspection required

### Certification Process
1. Submit energy compliance pathway to NJHMFA in LIHTC application
2. Register project with EPA ENERGY STAR partner
3. Pre-construction design review by HERS Rater
4. Mid-construction inspection (framing, insulation, air sealing)
5. Final performance testing (blower door, duct leakage)
6. Certification issued; submit to NJHMFA before CO request

### NJ Affordable Green Program
- Long-running NJHMFA incentive program (since 2000)
- Supports green materials and technologies in affordable housing
- 2,400+ units financed through program
- Information: https://www.nj.gov/dca/hmfa/developers/lihtc/greenpoints/

## General Developer Standards (All NJHMFA Programs)

### Site and Location
- Site must meet NJHMFA marketability standards
- Proximity to transit, employment, grocery, healthcare, schools evaluated
- Environmental review required (Phase I; Phase II if contamination suspected)
- Flood zone compliance (NJDEP, FEMA)

### Design and Construction
- Comply with NJ UCC, RSIS, all local zoning
- Accessibility: FHA 7 requirements (if 4+ units), Section 504 (if federally funded)
- ENERGY STAR certification (LIHTC — mandatory)
- Durable materials; 40-year design life minimum for new construction

### Financial Feasibility
- Demonstrated project viability through underwriting
- Debt service coverage ratio: typically ≥1.15–1.20
- Operating expense projections must be realistic
- Replacement reserve: minimum $300–$400/unit/year (may vary by program)

### Affirmative Marketing
- Affirmative Fair Housing Marketing Plan (AFHMP) required
- Must be approved by NJHMFA before lease-up
- Outreach to protected classes and underserved communities
- Document all marketing activities

### Developer Experience and Track Record
- Competitive advantage for developers with prior affordable housing completions
- NJHMFA may require experienced development consultant or co-developer for first-time applicants

## LIHTC Compliance Monitoring

### NJHMFA Annual Monitoring
- Annual rent rolls submitted to NJHMFA
- Annual income certifications for all tenants
- Physical inspection every 3 years minimum
- Unit transfers and vacancies tracked

### Tenant Income Recertification
- Annual recertification within 120 days of lease anniversary
- Third-party income verification required
- Over-income households: "next-available-unit rule" applies (IRS § 42(g)(2)(D)(ii))
  - Next unit of comparable or smaller size must be rented to income-eligible tenant
  - Over-income tenants may stay but next available unit must go to qualified tenant

### Non-Compliance
- NJHMFA reports non-compliance to IRS (Form 8823)
- IRS may assess credit recapture + interest + penalties
- Corrective action period: 90–180 days depending on violation type

## Federal LIHTC Income Limits (Annual Reference)

Income limits published annually by HUD. NJHMFA publishes NJ-specific tables by county:
- 50% AMI table: used for 20/50 test; HOME minimum income units
- 60% AMI table: used for 40/60 test (most LIHTC projects)
- 80% AMI table: used for moderate-income units (UHAC/COAH)
- 30% AMI table: used for very-low-income set-asides (Section 8, HOME, UHAC)

Current AMI tables: https://www.huduser.gov/portal/datasets/il.html
