# Architectural Contract Review Skill

Review architectural contracts against AIA standards and other industry benchmarks, identify unfair or risky clauses, and provide actionable feedback with recommended edits.

## Workflow

Make a todo list for all tasks in this workflow and work through them sequentially.

### 1. Intake: Gather Contract and Project Context

Before reviewing, collect what you need:

**From the contract itself (read carefully):**
- Contract type (Owner-Architect, Owner-Contractor, Architect-Consultant, etc.)
- Parties and their roles
- Project description and location
- Scope of services or work
- Fee and payment structure
- Key dates and milestones

**If the contract lacks critical context, ask the user:**
- What is the project type (residential, commercial, institutional, healthcare, etc.)?
- What is the approximate construction cost or budget?
- Is this a public or private project?
- What phase is the project in (pre-design, SD, DD, CD, CA)?
- Which party is the user representing (owner, architect, contractor, consultant)?
- Has there been prior work or a prior agreement with this party?
- What jurisdiction/state governs the project?
- Is the contract a modified standard form or entirely custom?

### 3. Clause-by-Clause Review

Analyze every substantive clause against the applicable standard. For each deviation, note whether it is **Minor**, **Moderate**, or **Critical** risk.

#### Scope of Services
- Is scope clearly defined and complete for this project phase?
- Are exclusions (surveys, geotechnical, permits, specialty consultants) explicitly stated?
- Is additional services mechanism present and clearly triggered?
- **Red flags**: Vague scope language that expands at no cost; scope that includes contractor obligations; open-ended "as needed" language with no fee adjustment.

#### Compensation and Payment
- Is the fee structure appropriate (fixed fee, hourly NTE, percentage of construction cost, or hybrid)?
- Are payment intervals defined (monthly, milestone-based)?
- Is there an invoice review period and a required time to pay?
- Is there an interest/late payment provision?
- Does additional services compensation have a clear authorization mechanism?
- **Red flags**: No late payment interest; payment contingent on owner's financing ("pay-if-financed"); no provision for fee adjustment on scope increases; no reimbursable expense structure; retainage on professional services (uncommon and problematic).

#### Ownership of Documents and Intellectual Property
- Who owns instruments of service (drawings, specs, models)?
- Is there a license grant to the owner for use on this project only?
- Is there language protecting against reuse on other projects without architect's permission?
- **Red flags**: Full copyright transfer (vs. license); license that survives termination for cause; owner right to modify documents without architect's consent; BIM model ownership transferred outright.

#### Standard of Care
- Does the agreement state the standard as "ordinary and reasonable care consistent with the profession" or equivalent?
- **Red flags**: "Best efforts," "ensure," "guarantee," or "warranty" language — these impose a higher-than-professional standard; fitness-for-purpose warranties on design.

#### Limitation of Liability
- Is there a mutual cap on liability (typically set to the greater of total fees paid or a fixed dollar amount)?
- Does it apply to both parties?
- **Red flags**: No liability cap; cap that applies only to architect, not owner; cap below total fees paid; carve-out that eliminates the cap for "willful misconduct" defined too broadly.

#### Indemnification
- Is indemnification mutual and proportional (each party indemnifies to the extent of its own negligence)?
- **Red flags**: Unilateral indemnification requiring architect to indemnify owner for ALL claims regardless of fault; broad indemnification including owner's own negligence; no carve-out for owner's independent negligence; indemnification that negates the liability cap.

#### Insurance
- What coverages and limits are required?
- Is professional liability (E&O) required, and are limits reasonable for the project scale?
- Is a certificate of insurance sufficient, or are additional insured endorsements required?
- **Red flags**: Named insured or additional insured on professional liability (problematic for occurrence-based policies); unreasonably high limits relative to fees; tail coverage requirements that are impractical; owner retaining right to approve insurer.

#### Termination
- Is there a termination for convenience provision for both parties?
- Is compensation upon termination (fees earned + wind-down costs) defined?
- Is there a cure period before termination for cause?
- **Red flags**: Termination for convenience with no compensation beyond work performed to date (cuts off additional services, wind-down, and cancellation fees); no cure period for alleged breach; owner can terminate and reuse documents for free.

#### Dispute Resolution
- Is there a mandatory mediation step before litigation or arbitration?
- Is arbitration voluntary or mandatory? Is the venue specified?
- Is there a statute of limitations provision?
- **Red flags**: Binding arbitration that removes right to appeal; venue in a distant jurisdiction; no mediation step; unreasonably short or long limitations period; jury trial waiver.

#### Waiver of Consequential Damages
- Is there a mutual waiver of consequential damages (lost profits, lost revenue, loss of use)?
- **Red flags**: One-sided waiver that only protects the owner; no waiver at all (exposes architect to unlimited consequential damages); carve-outs that swallow the rule.

#### Schedule and Time
- Are key milestone dates listed?
- Is the architect's schedule dependent on timely owner decisions and approvals?
- Is there force majeure / excusable delay language?
- **Red flags**: Hard dates with liquidated damages for architect delay; no extension for owner-caused delays; no provision for schedule impact if project is suspended.

#### Suspension and Restart
- Can the owner suspend work? For how long?
- Is the architect entitled to additional compensation for remobilization after suspension?
- **Red flags**: Unlimited owner suspension with no additional compensation; no right for architect to terminate after prolonged suspension.

#### BIM and Digital Data (if applicable)
- Is there an AIA E203 or equivalent exhibit attached?
- Are responsibilities for the BIM execution plan defined?
- **Red flags**: Owner demanding native BIM files (vs. published formats) without an interoperability agreement; contractor relying on architect's model as a construction document substitute; no disclaimer on model accuracy for contractor quantity takeoff.

#### Sustainable Design (if applicable)
- Is an AIA E204 or equivalent exhibit attached?
- Is LEED or other certification defined as a goal vs. a guarantee?
- **Red flags**: Guaranteed LEED certification level; architect liable for certification denial; sustainability obligations not tied to owner-provided budget.

#### Governing Law and Jurisdiction
- What state law governs?
- Is venue specified?
- **Red flags**: Governing law in a state with no connection to the project or parties; venue in owner's home city only; conflicting jurisdiction clauses.

#### Lien Rights (Architect-Consultant context)
- Does the architect retain the right to file a mechanics lien for unpaid fees?
- **Red flags**: Architect waives lien rights as a contract condition; pre-project lien waivers.

### 4. Comparative Gap Analysis

After clause review, produce a gap table comparing the submitted contract to the applicable AIA standard:

```
| Clause             | AIA Standard Provision        | Contract Provision      | Deviation | Risk     |
|--------------------|-------------------------------|-------------------------|-----------|----------|
| Indemnification    | Mutual, proportionate fault   | Unilateral, all claims  | Material  | Critical |
| Limitation of Liability | Mutual cap at fees paid  | No cap                  | Absent    | Critical |
| Payment terms      | 30 days, 1.5% monthly interest | 45 days, no interest   | Minor     | Low      |
...
```

### 5. Draft Recommended Edits

For each **Moderate** or **Critical** risk item, provide:
1. **Issue**: What the current clause says and why it's problematic
2. **Recommended language**: Specific replacement or addition text
3. **Fallback position**: Minimum acceptable compromise if the other party resists

Use plain, professional language. Distinguish between legal advice (refer to counsel) and commercial/technical observations (you can advise directly).

## Key Principles

- **Always distinguish** legal advice (refer to attorney) from technical/commercial observations (within scope).
- **Party perspective matters**: flag clauses that are one-sided against the user's party more prominently.
- **Project scale matters**: a $500K residential addition has different risk tolerance than a $50M hospital.
- **Jurisdiction matters**: some states have anti-indemnity statutes, contractor licensing requirements, or mandatory arbitration rules that supersede contract language.
- **Do not approve or certify contracts**: provide analysis and recommendations, not legal sign-off.
- **Ask before assuming**: if the contract is ambiguous about project type, delivery method, or scope, ask rather than guess.
