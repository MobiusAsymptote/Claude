# Accessibility Reference: FHA, Section 504, ADA, and ANSI A117.1

## Fair Housing Act — Design & Construction Requirements

### Citation
- 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(C)
- 24 CFR Part 100, Subpart D, § 100.205
- Effective date: March 13, 1991 (for first occupancy)

### Administering Agency
- **U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), FHEO**
- Web: https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp/accessibility_design
- **U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ)** (joint enforcement)

### Coverage — What Buildings Are Subject

| Building Type | Units Covered |
|---|---|
| Elevator building, 4+ units | **All** dwelling units |
| Non-elevator building, 4+ units | **Ground floor** dwelling units only |
| Building with fewer than 4 units | Not covered |
| Single-family homes | Not covered |

- "First occupancy" after March 13, 1991 triggers coverage
- Substantial rehabilitation may re-trigger FHA design requirements

### The Seven Design & Construction Requirements

**Requirement 1: Accessible Building Entrance on an Accessible Route**
- At least one building entrance must be on an accessible route
- Accessible route: level, stable, firm surface; slope ≤1:20; width ≥36″
- Exception: terrain or unusual characteristics make accessible entrance impractical (narrow exception)

**Requirement 2: Accessible and Usable Public and Common Use Areas**
Includes (not exhaustive):
- Parking lots and garages
- Building entrances and lobbies
- Hallways and corridors
- Elevators
- Mail rooms
- Laundry facilities
- Trash rooms
- Recreational facilities (pools, fitness rooms, courtyards)
- Rental and management offices
- Common restrooms

**Requirement 3: Usable Doors**
- 32″ minimum clear width (measured between face of door and stop when door is open 90°)
- Lever or loop hardware (no round knobs)
- Adequate maneuvering clearance on latch side
- Door closers set to allow 3-second close time minimum

**Requirement 4: Accessible Route Into and Through the Dwelling Unit**
- Continuous accessible path from entrance door through all habitable rooms
- No steps within the unit
- Thresholds: ≤¾″ (beveled if ½″–¾″)

**Requirement 5: Light Switches, Outlets, Thermostats, and Environmental Controls in Accessible Locations**
- Operable controls: 15″–48″ AFF (above finished floor)
- GFCI outlets in bathrooms and kitchens must be reachable
- Thermostat and HVAC controls within reach range

**Requirement 6: Reinforced Walls in Bathrooms for Grab Bars**
- Walls in all bathrooms must be reinforced to allow future installation of grab bars
- Reinforcement: blocking, 2×6 backing, or solid board within wall cavity
- Must support 250 lbs. at any point
- Grab bars not required to be installed at construction; reinforcement required

**Requirement 7: Usable Kitchens and Bathrooms**
Kitchen:
- Sufficient maneuvering space for wheelchair users at all appliances and sink
- U-shaped kitchen: ≥60″ turning space or one knee/toe clearance at base cabinets
- Standard kitchen: ≥30″×48″ clear floor space parallel or perpendicular to each appliance

Bathroom:
- ≥30″×48″ clear floor space at each fixture (toilet, sink, tub/shower)
- Tub/shower: clear floor space adjacent for transfer
- Option A (parallel approach at tub/shower side): 30″×60″ clear
- Option B (perpendicular approach): specific dimensions in FHA Design Manual

### FHA Safe Harbors
Any of the following demonstrates compliance with FHA design requirements:

1. HUD Fair Housing Accessibility Guidelines (March 6, 1991; Federal Register Vol. 56 No. 44)
2. Fair Housing Act Design Manual (HUD, 1996; updated 1998)
3. ANSI A117.1-1986 (with conditions)
4. ANSI A117.1-2003 or 2009 (with conditions)
5. ANSI A117.1-2017
6. ICC/ANSI A117.1-1998
7. IBC 2000 with 2001 Supplement (Ch. 11)
8. IBC 2003 through IBC 2018 (Ch. 11, with FHA modifications)
9. HUD-approved state or local accessibility standards

## Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act

### Citation
- 29 U.S.C. § 794
- Implementing regulation: 24 CFR Part 8
- Updated: April 25, 2023 (Federal Register)

### Administering Agency
- **U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD)**
- Web: https://www.hud.gov/504
- Regional HUD Offices enforce compliance

### Trigger: Federal Financial Assistance
Section 504 applies to any housing receiving HUD financial assistance including:
- HOME Investment Partnerships Program
- Community Development Block Grant (CDBG)
- Section 8 Project-Based Rental Assistance
- Section 202 (Elderly) / Section 811 (Disability)
- Public housing
- Choice Neighborhoods grants
- Capital Magnet Fund
- Low-Income Housing Tax Credits (when accompanied by federal subsidy)

### Unit Accessibility Requirements — New Construction

| Unit Type | Minimum | Technical Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Mobility-accessible units | **5% of total units** (min. 1) | ANSI A117.1 Type A or UFAS |
| Sensory-accessible units | **2% of total units** (min. 1) | Visual alarms, notification, TTY |

Calculation: Always round **up** to nearest whole unit. Minimum is 1 unit even for very small projects.

Example:
- 20-unit building: 5% = 1.0 → 1 mobility unit + 1 sensory unit
- 100-unit building: 5% = 5 mobility + 2 sensory
- 42-unit building: 5% = 2.1 → 3 mobility + 1 sensory

### Technical Standard Options
Developer or agency may choose either:
1. **UFAS** (Uniform Federal Accessibility Standards) — older standard; still compliant
2. **2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design** — current preferred standard

ANSI A117.1-2017 Type A unit also satisfies Section 504 mobility-accessible unit requirements.

### Alterations (Non-Substantial)
- Alterations to units must be made accessible to maximum extent feasible
- Continue until 5% mobility-accessible threshold is reached
- No minimum percentage requirement for sensory units during alterations
- Substantial rehabilitation: treated as new construction

### Program Accessibility (Existing Buildings)
- Programs and services must be accessible even if all buildings are not
- May use administrative, structural, or other means to achieve program accessibility

## Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)

### Citation
- 42 U.S.C. § 12101 et seq.
- 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design (28 CFR Part 36, Appendix C)

### Administering Agency
- **U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ)**
- Web: https://www.ada.gov
- **U.S. Access Board** (technical standards)
- Web: https://www.access-board.gov/ada/

### ADA and Residential Housing — Scope

| Building/Use | ADA Applicability |
|---|---|
| Private residential apartments/condos (not government) | **Does NOT apply** to units; FHA applies |
| Common areas open to public (rental office, leasing center) | **Title III applies** |
| Government-owned public housing | **Title II applies** (all common areas) |
| Student/transitional/emergency housing (government) | **Title II applies** |
| Nursing homes, assisted living (public accommodations) | **Title III applies** |
| Private club housing | Limited Title III |

**Key principle**: ADA does not govern individual dwelling units in private residential buildings. The Fair Housing Act governs unit accessibility.

### ADA Title III — Common Areas
- Rental offices and leasing centers must be fully accessible
- On-site retail, gyms, pool facilities open to non-residents must comply
- New construction or alterations must meet 2010 ADA Standards
- Existing facilities: "readily achievable" barrier removal required

### ADA Title II — Government Housing
- Public housing authorities must ensure all programs and services are accessible
- New construction: full accessibility per 2010 ADA Standards
- Alterations: path of travel to altered area must be accessible

## ANSI A117.1-2017 (NJ UCC Reference Standard)

### Publisher
- **International Code Council (ICC)**
- Web: https://www.iccsafe.org
- NJ UCC online viewer: https://up.codes/viewer/new_jersey/icc-a117.1-2017

### Unit Type Hierarchy

| Type | Applicability | Maneuverability |
|---|---|---|
| **Accessible Unit** | Public accommodations; highest standard | Full wheelchair; largest clearances |
| **Type A Dwelling Unit** | Section 504-funded (5% units); COAH townhouses | Full wheelchair; removable base cabinets |
| **Type B Dwelling Unit** | FHA-covered buildings (4+ units) | Basic/aging-in-place; aligns with FHA 7 requirements |
| **Type C (Visitable)** | Voluntary visitability standard | Single accessible entry, ground-floor half bath, wide doors |

### Type A vs. Type B Key Differences

| Feature | Type A | Type B (FHA-aligned) |
|---|---|---|
| Kitchen base cabinets | Removable; 30″×48″ knee space | Maneuvering clearance only |
| Kitchen clear floor space | 60″ turning diameter | 30″×48″ approach space |
| Bathroom clear floor space | Enhanced; 60″ turning in lavatory area | 30″×48″ at each fixture |
| Roll-in shower | Required in one bathroom | Not required |
| Turning radius (2017 ed.) | 67″ diameter (2017) / 60″ (2009) | Same update |
| Push-side door clearance | 52″ length (2017) / 48″ (2009) | Same update |

### 2017 Edition Changes from 2009

| Element | 2009 | 2017 |
|---|---|---|
| Turning radius | 60″ diameter | 67″ diameter (reduced overlap to 10″) |
| Push-side door clearance | 48″ | 52″ |
| Interior clear width | 32″ allowed for short runs | 32″ only every 52″ apart |
| New additions | — | EV charging, water bottle filling, acoustic standards |

NJ UCC currently references **A117.1-2017**.

## NJ Law Against Discrimination (LAD)

### Citation
- N.J.S.A. 10:5-1 et seq.
- Enforced by: NJ Division on Civil Rights (DCR), Office of the Attorney General
- Web: https://www.njoag.gov/dcr/

### Protected Classes (Broader than Federal FHA)

| Protected Class | Federal FHA | NJ LAD |
|---|---|---|
| Race, color, national origin | ✓ | ✓ |
| Sex | ✓ | ✓ |
| Familial status | ✓ | ✓ |
| Disability | ✓ | ✓ |
| Religion / Creed | ✓ | ✓ |
| Sexual orientation | ✓ (2012 HUD rule) | ✓ |
| Gender identity/expression | ✓ (2012 HUD rule) | ✓ |
| Source of income (Section 8) | ✗ | ✓ (key NJ addition) |
| Ancestry | ✗ | ✓ |
| Marital status | ✗ | ✓ |
| Age | Limited | ✓ |

**Source of income protection** is a significant NJ-specific provision: landlords in NJ may not refuse Section 8 / Housing Choice Vouchers.

### Filing a Complaint
- DCR complaint deadline: **180 days** from alleged discrimination
- Alternative: NJ Superior Court (Law Division) or HUD within 1 year

## Visitability Standards

### Definition
Visitability is a voluntary design standard ensuring all new homes have basic features enabling people with disabilities to visit and use the ground level.

### Core Visitability Elements
1. Zero-step or ramped entrance (at least one)
2. All main-floor interior doorways ≥32″ clear (36″ preferred)
3. One accessible half-bath on ground floor
4. Reinforced walls for grab bar installation
5. Accessible route within ground-floor living space

### NJ-Specific Context
- No statewide visitability mandate for all new construction
- COAH-credited multistory townhouses must meet Type A features (adaptable entrance, first-floor bath and bedroom)
- Some municipal zoning ordinances include visitability requirements
- NJHMFA QAP awards competitive points for accessibility beyond minimum requirements

### Comparison to Binding Standards

| Standard | Binding? | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| FHA 7 requirements | Yes (4+ unit buildings) | All covered units |
| Section 504 (5%/2%) | Yes (federally funded) | 5%/2% of units |
| ANSI A117.1 Type A | Yes (where mandated) | Designated accessible units |
| Visitability | Voluntary | Any home |
